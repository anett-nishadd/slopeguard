01 THE IDEA

A concept shaped by real-world need

Small signals.
Thoughtful response.

Inspired by the 30 July 2024 Chooralmala–Mundakkai disaster in Wayanad, Kerala, SLOPEGUARD is a proposed monitoring-and-alert concept for slope-risk areas.

It combines measured ground conditions with rainfall information to help trained authorities identify concerning changes early. It does not claim to predict every landslide.

i This is a student prototype concept—not a certified or operational warning system.

03 THE WORKFLOW

A careful sequence

Measure.
Review. Respond.

Reliable monitoring starts well before an alert is ever considered.

  1. 01

    Assess the slope

    Geologists and geotechnical engineers evaluate the site and choose suitable sensor locations.

  2. 02

    Install & establish a baseline

    Calibrate professional instruments and learn what normal conditions look like over time.

  3. 03

    Record & compare

    Track rainfall, ground-water response, and measurable movement against validated site thresholds.

  4. 04

    Verify & communicate

    A staffed team checks unusual patterns; authorised officials decide on public warnings or evacuation instructions.

05 TESTING & VALIDATION

Prove the prototype
before field use.

Start with safe, controlled bench tests. Treat every threshold and alert as a simulation until professionals validate the system for a specific site.

01 / BASELINE

Bench & calibration

Check sensor readings against known or reference conditions. Record timestamps, expected ranges, and calibration results.

02 / SENSOR FAULT

Disconnect a sensor

Simulate missing or invalid readings. Confirm the dashboard identifies a sensor fault instead of showing a normal state.

03 / NETWORK

Interrupt connectivity

Test Wi-Fi or telemetry loss. Check that the fault is visible and local demo logic behaves as designed during an outage.

04 / POWER

Check backup power

Verify the power-failure indication and approved backup behavior without compromising electrical safety.

05 / RELIABILITY

Run longer trials

Observe drift, missing records, communication interruptions, and maintenance needs over extended supervised operation.

Only in an authorised, expert-supervised pilot: collect data across weather conditions, compare sensor records with field observations, measure false alarms and missed events, and document maintenance and communication failures. Thresholds must be validated for that site—not copied from a demo.

06 FROM CLASSROOM TO FIELD

Progress with
permission & proof.

Any real-world application needs expert collaboration, measured validation, and formal approval.

STAGE 01

Student prototype

Demonstrate the logic on a stable tabletop model. Mark every alert as a simulation.

STAGE 02

Supervised pilot

Seek collaboration with qualified geologists, a university, and relevant authorities. Survey first; install only with permission.

STAGE 03

Validation

Compare sensor data with expert observations across conditions; measure false alarms and missed events, then document failures.

STAGE 04

Operational proposal

Require independent technical review, resilient communications, response procedures, and formal approval.

07 LIMITATIONS & SAFETY

One sensor can’t
promise safety.

A wetness sensor is not a calibrated rain gauge. Soil moisture is not the same as pore-water pressure. Tilt alone cannot confirm slope failure, and no single sensor guarantees prediction.

Never destabilise a real slope or put people at risk during tests. SLOPEGUARD must not trigger evacuations or replace official warnings.
